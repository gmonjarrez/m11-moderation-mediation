Moderation and mediation

Baron and Kenny (1986) distinguish two ways a third variable can help explain a relationship. A moderator identifies the conditions under which an effect changes. A mediator represents a process through which the predictor may influence the outcome.

Moderation Mediation
Research question When or for whom does the effect differ? How or why does the effect occur?
Role of the third variable Changes the strength or direction of the relationship Carries part or all of the effect through an indirect path
Main quantity Interaction coefficient Indirect effect, a × b

Testing moderation

In a linear regression, include the predictor (X), moderator (Z), and their product (X × Z). The product coefficient tests whether the slope of X changes with Z. Compare the slopes directly using the interaction term. If the interaction is supported, report the estimated effect of X at meaningful values of Z (Baron & Kenny, 1986, pp. 1174–1176).

Testing mediation

Baron and Kenny’s original procedure estimates three regressions (1986, pp. 1176–1178).

  1. Predict the mediator (M) from X to estimate path a.
  2. Predict the outcome (Y) from X to estimate the total effect c.
  3. Predict Y from X and M together to estimate path b and the direct effect c′.

In their framework, a, c, and b should be supported in the expected directions, and c′ should be smaller in magnitude than c. A remaining direct effect is consistent with partial mediation, and a zero direct effect corresponds to complete mediation in the model. The article also describes Sobel’s approximate test of a × b. The causal interpretation depends on the proposed direction from M to Y. Measurement error in M can weaken its estimated role.

Application to customer research

Suppose I test whether a redesigned restaurant reservation page increases completed bookings.

  • Moderator. Device type could change the effect of the redesign. For example, the new layout might help mobile visitors more than desktop visitors. I would test the interaction between page version and device type.
  • Mediator. The redesign could increase perceived ease of booking, which could in turn increase completions. I would measure perceived ease after visitors see the page and before they decide whether to book.

The article makes a similar distinction in its discussion of crowding. The option to leave a room could change how density affects crowding. Perceived control could explain how density leads people to feel crowded. A single study can test both roles by manipulating the option to leave and measuring perceived control (Baron & Kenny, 1986, pp. 1178–1180).
